# Answer key: hosted Postgres pricing for a small UK startup

How the list was chosen: I read 9 independent 2025-26 comparison articles and the Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey. A provider is **core** if at least 4 of the 9 comparisons cover it, or if 3 do and the survey lists it as a widely used cloud platform. The three hyperscaler flagships count as core on the same test.
Everything else that appears in the comparisons or in the brief is **extra**. Each extra row says why it fell short. Every price, free tier and region below was checked on 27 Sep 2026 against the provider's own pricing page, docs or official price API. The comparison articles were used only to choose the list.

Question: "compare the pricing of the main hosted Postgres providers for a small UK startup, and tell me which to pick."

In quotes, line breaks are collapsed to spaces and em dashes are written as hyphens. Everything else is as the source prints it. All prices exclude VAT. "$" means USD as the provider states it. Monthly figures marked "=" are my arithmetic from the quoted hourly rate at 730 hours, not the provider's own figure.

## Facts a correct recommendation should rest on

1. **UK law does not require the data to sit in the UK.** Under UK GDPR, sending data to the EEA is a restricted transfer, but "All the countries in the European Economic Area (EEA) have full adequacy". The US has only "Partial adequacy", which covers transfers under the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. So Frankfurt or Amsterdam is lawful. A London region is about latency, customer contracts or policy, not a legal requirement. A report that says UK GDPR forces a London region is wrong. Source: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/adequacy-regulations/is-the-restricted-transfer-covered-by-adequacy-regulations/
2. **Which providers offer London.**
   - Yes: RDS and Aurora (eu-west-2), Cloud SQL and AlloyDB (europe-west2), Azure (UK South), Supabase, Neon, DigitalOcean (LON1), Aiven, Crunchy Bridge, PlanetScale, Tiger Cloud and Fly (lhr).
   - No: Render (Frankfurt nearest), Railway (Amsterdam), Nile and Xata (Frankfurt), Prisma (Paris/Frankfurt), and self-serve Heroku (London is Private Spaces only).
   - Sources are the region links in the table.
3. **Supabase's "Europe" region is not London.** The general region "includes London and Zurich". A startup needing UK location must choose "West Europe (London) eu-west-2" explicitly. Source: https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions
4. **Free tiers end in different ways, and that matters more than their size.**
   - Supabase pauses after 1 week of inactivity, and allows 2 active projects.
   - Neon always scales to zero, gives 0.5 GB per project, and from 5 Oct 2026 may delete Free projects inactive for 90+ days.
   - Render deletes access 30 days after creation.
   - Aiven Free powers off when inactive, and you cannot choose the region.
   - AWS's new Free plan is credits that close the account after 6 months.
   - GCP's Cloud SQL trial instance lasts 30 days.
   - Azure gives 12 months of B1MS.
   - DigitalOcean, PlanetScale, Crunchy Bridge, Fly, Tiger, Xata and Heroku have no free database tier.
   - Sources are the free-tier links in the table.
5. **Price cliffs from free to paid, and from paid to the next tier.**
   - Supabase: $0, then $25 (Pro, with $10 compute credit), then $599 (Team, which adds SOC2/ISO 27001 and SSO).
   - Neon: $0, then usage with no minimum. About $20/month always-on at 0.25 CU, plus storage.
   - Render: $0, then $6.
   - Aiven: $0, then $5 (no region choice), then $12 (region choice), then $75 (first SLA).
   - Crunchy Bridge: Hobby is not for production, so the real entry is $70 (US East) or $87.50 (London).
   - Sources: https://supabase.com/pricing , https://neon.com/pricing , https://render.com/pricing , https://aiven.io/pricing?product=pg , https://docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/plans-pricing
6. **The cheapest paid London options sit in a narrow band. The differences are in what you get, not in price.**
   - Roughly, per month: PlanetScale PS-5 $5 (single node, 512 MiB), Aiven Hobbyist on DO London $11.68, Azure B1MS about £13.35 including storage, RDS db.t4g.micro about $15.80 with 20 GB, DigitalOcean $15.00-15.15, Neon about $20 always-on, and Supabase $25 (which also bundles auth, storage and APIs).
   - Sources are the table rows.
7. **Several cheapest tiers carry no SLA or are labelled not-for-production.**
   - Cloud SQL shared-core is "not covered by the Cloud SQL SLA".
   - AlloyDB 1 vCPU is "for development and testing purposes only".
   - Crunchy Hobby is "not intended for production usage".
   - Aiven's SLA starts at Startup.
   - Heroku Essential "can tolerate up to 4 hours of downtime per month".
   - Sources: https://cloud.google.com/sql/pricing , https://docs.cloud.google.com/alloydb/docs/cluster-create , https://docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/plans-pricing , https://aiven.io/pricing?product=pg , https://devcenter.heroku.com/articles/heroku-postgres-plans
8. **Currency.** Every developer platform here prices in USD. Azure's retail price API quotes UK South directly in GBP (£0.014/hr for B1MS). A UK startup paying in USD carries exchange-rate exposure. Source: https://prices.azure.com/api/retail/prices
9. **Heroku is not a forward-looking pick.** On 6 Feb 2026 Heroku began "transitioning to a sustaining engineering model ... rather than introducing new features". "Enterprise Account contracts will no longer be offered to new customers." Source: https://www.heroku.com/blog/an-update-on-heroku/
10. **Hyperscaler storage and HA are billed separately and double quickly.**
    - RDS Multi-AZ doubles the db.t4g.micro rate, from $0.018 to $0.036/hr in London.
    - DigitalOcean HA needs a second $30 node.
    - Crunchy Bridge HA "doubles the cluster price".
    - Sources: AWS Price List API eu-west-2 CSV, https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/pricing/ , https://docs.crunchybridge.com/concepts/plans-pricing

What a correct recommendation looks like: it names a London-capable provider or says outright that EU hosting is lawful (fact 1). It does not recommend Render, Railway or self-serve Heroku to a startup that needs UK location. It states the free-tier limits that stop a real app (fact 4) and the first paid step (fact 5). It separates "cheapest" from "production-suitable" (fact 7). A defensible default is Supabase Pro in London if the startup wants auth and APIs bundled, or Neon in London if it wants plain Postgres that scales to zero. DigitalOcean LON1, RDS eu-west-2 and Azure UK South are defensible fixed-price alternatives.
